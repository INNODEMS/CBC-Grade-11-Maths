    Key Takeaway 11.3.1.

    The increase in value of an asset over time is referred to as Appreciation.
    🔗
    Let the original value of the land be \(P\text{,}\) the new value be \(A\text{,}\) the increase in value be \(I\text{,}\) and the percentage increase be \(R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    From the learner experience, we can see that,
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A = \amp \; P + I \\ I = \amp \; \frac{R}{100} \times P \\ A = \amp \; P + \frac{R}{100} \times P \\ = \amp \; P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    For the second year the new value becomes the original value.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A = \amp \; P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right) + \frac{R}{100} \times P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \\ = \amp \; P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \\ = \amp \; P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    In general, after \(T\) years, the value of the asset can be calculated using the formula,
    \begin{equation*} A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.3.2.

    A piece of land in a developing suburban area is purchased for \(2,500,000\) Shillings. If the value of land in that area appreciates at a steady rate of \(15\%\) per annum, what will be the value of the land after \(6\) years?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given values
    🔗
    \begin{align*} P \amp =\; 2,500,000 \;\ text{shillings} \\ R \amp = \; 15 \% \\ T \amp = \; 6\;\text{years}\\ A \amp = \; ? \end{align*}
    🔗
    Apply the Appreciation formula, \(A = P(1 + R)^T\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A \amp =\; 2,500,000 (1 + \frac{15}{100})^6 \\ \amp = \;2,500,000 \times 2.31306 = 5,782,650 \text{ Shillings} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.3.3. Finding the Original Value (Principal).

    A company’s current value is \(4,200,000\) Shillings. It has been appreciating at a rate of \(8\%\) per year for the last \(5\) years. How much was the original value?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given values:
    \begin{align*} A \amp =\; 4,200,000 \text{ Shillings} \\ R \amp = \; 8\% \text{ p.a.} \\ T \amp = \; 5 \text{ years} \\ P \amp = \; ? \end{align*}
    🔗
    Make \(P\) the subject in the appreciation formula: \(P = \frac{A}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T}\)
    🔗
    Substitute the values into the adjusted formula:
    \begin{align*} P \amp = \; \frac{4,200,000}{\left(1 + \frac{8}{100}\right)^5} \\ \amp = \; \frac{4,200,000}{(1.08)^5} \\ \amp = \; \frac{4,200,000}{1.46933} \\ \amp \approx \; 2,858,448 \text{ Shillings} \end{align*}
    The original value of the company 5 years ago was approximately \(2,858,448\) Shillings.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.3.4. Calculating the Annual Appreciation Rate.

    A house was bought for \(120,000\) Shillings and sold \(10\) years later for \(311,200\) Shillings. At what annual rate did the house appreciate?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Identify the given values:
    \begin{align*} P \amp =\; 120,000 \text{ Shillings} \\ A \amp = \; 311,200 \text{ Shillings} \\ T \amp = \; 10 \text{ years} \\ R \amp = \; ? \end{align*}
    🔗
    Rearrange the appreciation formula to solve for \(R\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \frac{A}{P} \amp = \; \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T \\ \sqrt[T]{\frac{A}{P}} \amp = \; 1 + \frac{R}{100} \\ R \amp = \; 100 \times \left( \sqrt[T]{\frac{A}{P}} - 1\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Substitute the given values into your derived equation:
    \begin{align*} R \amp = \; 100 \times \left(\sqrt[10]{\frac{311,200}{120,000}} - 1\right)\\ \amp = \; 100 \times \left(\sqrt[10]{2.59333} - 1\right)\\ \amp = \; 100 \times ((1.1000) - 1)\\ \amp = \; 10\% \text{ per annum} \end{align*}
    The house appreciated at an annual rate of \(10\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
